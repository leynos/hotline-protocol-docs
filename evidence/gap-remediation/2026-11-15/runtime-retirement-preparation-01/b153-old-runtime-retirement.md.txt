runtime file changed. Retirement remains conditional on A192's current FD/free

## Small artefacts to preserve outside retired directories

Before removing any directory, inventory and copy with path/size/SHA into a
small provenance bundle (do not silently discard a unique receipt):

- All four `assets.json` files, plus existing `last-launch.json`,
  `machine.sheepvm/prefs` and `machine.sheepvm/nvram` where present. The NVRAM
  is a historical boot/config artefact, not a replacement for a disk.
- `hotline19/shared/Hotline19/source-manifest.json` and the small Finder/
  AppleDouble staging metadata or its already pinned source under
  `state/native-mac/shared`/`../hotline-bench`; preserve exact DATA/RSRC
  identities in the manifest. Do not treat shader caches as provenance.
- `hotline21/sheep-service.json` and `sheep-service.log`, plus all existing
  `last-launch.json`/prefs from `hotline19`/`20`/`21`. Preserve first-run
  failure/launch records already held in `state/native-mac` and committed
  evidence. Runtime-embedded emulator executables are pinned by build hashes
  and reproducible under `tools`, not unique guest state.
- Retain the original `state/native-mac/base-os9.dsk`, install archive and
  source ROM outside these runtime directories, subject to their own
  independent retention policy. Keep `hotline22-recovery/base.dsk` and its
  current working image; this decision does not affect other runtimes.

After copying, verify each small artefact's SHA against its source and record
the exact retired root/inode set, current FD-free/no-owner condition and XFS
exclusive blocks. If any acceptance script still opens an old `working.dsk`,
preserve its extracted output or update that script to portable evidence
before retirement. Do not claim a mutable old disk was byte-identical to the
new canonical base merely from its historical `assets.json`.
