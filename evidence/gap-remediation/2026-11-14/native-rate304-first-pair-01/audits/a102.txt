# A102: C107 first-pair timing boundary

Read-only audit of stopped `state/native-sessions/native-rate304-epoch-01`.
No VM, oracle, mxd, protocol request, or tracked edit. `coordinator/report.json`
and three PCAP lifetimes are authoritative; the initial C-worker statement
of zero 304/500 requests is contradicted by those bytes.

## What ran

The separate C106 control-only preflight completed all 18 plan actions,
including six numbered requests: 107, 348, 200, 206, 300, 303. The C107
case sent another six numbered requests: observer 107/300, paced subject
107/300, then exactly one 304 ID1000 icon2 and one 500 ID2000. No second
304, paced 26-pair trial, 70-second reset wait, burst subject, fresh ban
probe or retained observer controls ran. Case report status is `failed` with
`ValueError: Missing paced subject/observer301 and500 ACK icon2`.

The case recorder's action offsets from its start are 304 at 0.837840043 s
and 500 at 0.837876862 s. Its configured pair wait is **0.55 s** from
`update_and_barrier`; the failed wait triggered cleanup at about 1.388 s.
Observer `shutdown_write` occurred at 1.388185168 s and observer EOF at
1.395744858 s. The paced flow then received 302 UID2 (the observer's
departure) at 1.580642751 s, its own 301 UID3/name `fixture-low`/icon2 at
1.585970516 s, and its 500 ID2000 success ACK at 1.585992998 s. These
late frames are retained in the failed report, not evidence that the pair
passed before the wait expired. Paced EOF followed its own cleanup close.
The observer flow did not receive an icon2 301 before its FIN. No packet
shows a server RST. The observer departure and missing observer 301 cannot
be interpreted independently of the already-started client cleanup.

## Independent packets and limits

Namespace PCAP has three SYN lifetimes at source ports 53738 (preflight),
44482 (observer), 44496 (paced), all to 127.0.0.1:15880. The guest PCAP has
the corresponding same-source-port flows to 10.0.2.15:5500. Reassembly of
each direction is byte-equal between namespace and guest: lengths C→S/S→C
are 231/2759, 108/448 and 159/521 bytes respectively. Case recorder sent
and received streams equal the full reassembled 44482 and 44496 streams;
there are zero unread application bytes, no RST packets and FINs on both
case lifetimes. The namespace capture journal reports 75 packets, zero
kernel drops; the host primary SYN capture reports zero packets/drops.
These are packet observations, not a claim of full semantic case completion.

PCAP timing for the paced flow puts the 304 payload at +0.476745 s and 500
payload at +0.517769 s after its SYN. The 301 and ACK share a server packet
at +1.224748 s: about 0.748 s after 304 and 0.707 s after 500 reached the
namespace capture. This exceeds the runner's 0.55-second acceptance window.
The bytes alone do not identify why the server delayed or what would have
arrived on observer had the client not closed it.

The case unit exited 1 and is stopped; owned host/namespace capture and
lock units are inactive, as are the disposable bridge and emulator units.
Stopped admin and fixture-low UserData, Prefs, DATA/RSRC and board fork
hashes are retained in `stopped/forks.json`; no Banned file is reported.
This checks a clean failed-epoch boundary, not a 304 limiter outcome.

Smallest falsifiable timing hypothesis for later source review: first-pair
301/500 processing can take longer than the configured 0.55-second wait in
this isolated runtime. Its prediction is a late same-pair response without
a limiter error, as observed on the still-open paced flow. An independent
future supervised timing case with a declared longer wait would be needed
to distinguish recurring first-pair delay from a one-off scheduler/network
effect; this audit authorises no retune or live retry.
