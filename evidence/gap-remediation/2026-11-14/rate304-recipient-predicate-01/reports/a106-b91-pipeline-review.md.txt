## Result

No new pre-live blocker found in the scoped B91 changes. This is acceptance
of an offline prepared runner, not a native result or GAP-045 resolution.

- Source plan advances schedule schema to 2 and declares the 26-pair phase
  mode. `run_update_phase` sends each 304 before its same-subject numbered
  500, for IDs1000–1025/2000–2025 and 3000–3025/4000–4025. It sends all
  52 ordered requests, then drains both owned sockets with the existing
  select collector until each icon2..27 appears exactly once on subject
  and observer and each matching 500 ACK is present. It checks effective
  name, UID and icon after the readiness predicate. The pair loop retains
  the first send, last send, and required-arrival timestamps. It makes no
  total order claim between sockets.
- The 26-pair phase is bounded by <=20 **host** seconds including sends and
  required receptions; a send-window or completion overrun fails. The
  paced >=70-second subject quiet interval still has observer 500 cadence,
  followed by 304 icon28 plus 500 with an explicit <=5-second wait for
  both 301 views and ACK. The burst 27th 304 has no 500 and still requires
  finite EOF/RST plus one fresh valid107 temporary-ban error. The reporter
  preserves `native_clock_calibrated=false` and does not infer native
  60-second progression from host time.
- The observer remains open while each 26-pair phase drains, unlike C107's
  failed 0.55-second first-pair deadline followed by cleanup. Failure still
  closes only owned sockets in `run()`'s `finally`; late frames during
  cleanup may contribute to `phase_progress`, which the guide correctly
  labels observed partial bytes, not completed phase proof.
- `wire.identities` records a dynamic UID immediately after each verified
  subject roster, so a later failure can report reached identity stages.
  The successful report pins source/cases/plan/oracle hashes, exact actions,
  events, full streams, parsed and unparsed frames, terminals, 26/26 progress,
  phase mode and timing receipts. The verifier reconstructs sent/received
  bytes and checks phase timestamps/ACKs. It does not claim host/guest
  packet parity or stopped-fork proof; those remain parent capture gates.
- Request bounds are unchanged by pipelining: paced Login107+300+
  26*(304+500)+304+500 = 56 complete requests, burst 107+300+
  26*(304+500)+terminal304 = 55, observer Login107+300+13–15 idle500+
  five controls = 20–22, probe one107. Each is below the dispatcher
  >120 per-session threshold, subject to packet verification of no
  unplanned requests. At most three case sockets are simultaneous, plus
  a separately completed preflight lifetime. No account/file/option
  mutation or new dependency is introduced.

## Independent focused validation and residual limits

```sh

  python3 -m unittest discover -s scripts -p test_native_rate304.py -v
```

All **8 tests passed** in 6.074 s. The real `socketpair` rehearsal receives
all 52 sent frames and delays dual-socket 301/ACK replies by 0.75 seconds;
both observer-first and subject-first orders pass without observer closure.
Missing, duplicate, foreign update and sub-delay timeout variants fail;
the old 0.55-second pair wait fails before the late synthetic ACK. Fake
complete reports also exercise byte/timing/source/terminal faults. The
rehearsal models delayed responses; it cannot establish the server's
actual latency, recipient bit continuity, native clock, or ban outcome.

The guide's A103 sentence that the connection predicate was outside that
review is historically accurate but now superseded by A104/A105: the
recipient helper reads the handshake-set connection bit7 at +0x4c plus
session online. Parent documentation should link that newer bounded trace
when publishing, without inferring that C107's missing observer update
was caused solely by timeout.
