**Conclusion:** The unchanged GAP-030 closure criterion is met for the native 1.9 server and the observed/default ASCII cases, provided the parent accepts a shared-helper source trace plus representative 121/304 fixtures rather than requiring a 121 unknown-bit or disable matrix not named by the criterion. The canonical registry/progress currently predates A37 and still says 121 unresolved, so it needs a documentation/status reconciliation if root closes GAP-030. Do not claim arbitrary higher-bit semantics beyond the three assignments, all-input byte-width rules, generalized user-supplied 215 encoding, or cross-socket total ordering.

**Separate open questions:** GAP-029 explicitly asks session-only versus durable persistence and field-by-field update/default/persistence with before/after 301; re-login/restart durability is its own clause. GAP-018 explicitly asks non-ASCII, NUL, CR/LF and peer transformations across many text fields. Neither is an unspoken requirement of GAP-030. If root interprets “default text/encoding” to include *user-supplied* non-ASCII 215 bytes, that is an interpretation decision: the smallest further packet is one bounded 121 or 304 non-ASCII 215 case with exact bytes and default control, not a blanket all-field charset programme.
